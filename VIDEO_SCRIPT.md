# Scam Pause demo script

The captioned video (docs/demo-captioned.mp4, 2:46, no sound) shows these lines in order. Read them as a voice-over if you record one.

1. In 2025, Canadians reported $704 million lost to fraud. The Anti-Fraud Centre thinks only 5 to 10% of fraud is ever reported.
2. Most scams win in the same moment: someone wants money, a code or your card, and they want it right now.
3. Scam Pause is for that moment, for older adults, newcomers and the family who look out for them.
4. It runs on your device and keeps nothing you paste. A security policy stops the page from sending anything anywhere.
5. Here is a made up call from a "bank fraud department".
6. Stop. It names the scam: the bank investigator scam, and says honestly that no app can be certain from words alone.
7. It says what usually happens next, and how to check safely: hang up and call the number on the back of your card.
8. Every warning sign is in plain words, with the words that triggered it and the Canadian source behind it.
9. A real bank never asks you to move money to a safe account, keep it from your branch, or stay on the line.
10. Then it buys time. The Anti-Fraud Centre suggests hanging up and waiting ten minutes before calling your bank back.
11. The ten minute pause comes with a checklist. It is sped up here for the video.
12. If it was real, it is still real after ten minutes. Scams can't wait. That is the point.
13. Ask someone you trust. A second person breaks the spell.
14. The message is written for you, with links, codes and card numbers taken out so it is safe to forward. Text or call them in one tap.
15. On a call, there's no text to paste. Answer three quick questions instead.
16. The CRA does not take gift cards and does not threaten arrest. Scam Pause says so, with the CRA's own page as the source.
17. Links are checked without opening them: lookalike names, disguised letters and unusual endings.
18. A real bank alert gets no warnings. Even then it says plainly that this doesn't prove a message is safe.
19. Already sent money? Clear steps, in order: your bank first, then the police and the Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre.
20. Plain rules, no AI model, works offline. Tested on 96 made up messages, including 24 written after the rules were frozen.
21. And it is clear about its limits: it reads words, not voices, and it is not an emergency service.
22. Scam Pause. Before you send money, pause. Made by John Khan in Toronto, Ontario.
